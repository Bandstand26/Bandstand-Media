Protecting Student Privacy While Promoting Your Band

BandStand Media · November 28, 2026

Promoting a marching band should never require sacrificing student privacy. Because many ensembles include minors, directors and media teams need a clear process for deciding what can be captured, published and retained.

Understand the institutional rules

Review school media-release procedures and applicable privacy requirements with administrators. In the United States, FERPA can apply to education records, including certain photographs or videos depending on context; it does not make every public performance image an education record. Follow local policy and obtain guidance when uncertain.

Minimize information exposure

Avoid publishing student IDs, private contact information, medical details, grades or individualized disciplinary information. Public event schedules may be appropriate, but private travel itineraries and real-time lodging locations can create safety risks.

Manage consent operationally

Maintain an approved process for identifying students who should not be featured without exposing their status publicly. Review footage before publication and crop or omit material where necessary. Provide a clear channel for families to raise concerns.

Secure production systems

Store footage in authorized accounts, limit access by role and use multifactor authentication. Define retention and deletion procedures. Do not move student records into personal cloud accounts or unapproved editing services.

Respond to incidents

If sensitive information is posted accidentally, restrict access or remove the post promptly, notify the appropriate school official and follow institutional incident procedures. Preserve necessary records securely rather than discussing the affected student publicly.

Train the team

Use a prepublication checklist covering consent, location, rights, captions and sensitive information. Student media volunteers should understand the rules but adults must retain responsibility for serious decisions. Privacy protection is part of professional media practice.
