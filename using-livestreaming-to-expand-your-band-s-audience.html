Using Livestreaming to Expand Your Band's Audience

BandStand Media · November 28, 2026

Livestreaming can bring performances to alumni, families and fans who cannot attend in person. It also creates technical, rights and moderation responsibilities that should be addressed before the broadcast begins.

Confirm the event is suitable

Obtain venue and institutional permission and determine whether music and recording rights cover live transmission and replay. Decide whether the event should be public, restricted or not streamed at all. Consider student privacy and any contractual broadcast restrictions.

Plan the technical setup

Test upload bandwidth at the actual venue, choose a stable camera position and monitor audio. Prepare power supplies and a local recording if permitted. Assign one operator to watch stream health rather than expecting the director to troubleshoot during performance.

Design the viewing experience

Publish the start time and explain the event format. Use readable titles and provide captions for spoken material where feasible. Avoid showing private backstage areas or information that could expose students to unnecessary risk.

Moderate actively

Assign an adult moderator to manage chat, remove abusive comments and escalate serious concerns. Consider disabling chat if adequate moderation is unavailable. Explain how viewers can report technical issues without contacting students directly.

Example: a virtual concert

A program might test its stream a week before the concert, publish an official viewing link, run a short pre-event check and record locally as a backup. If the connection fails, communicate clearly and provide an authorized replay only if rights allow.

Review results

Examine concurrent viewers, watch duration, technical interruptions and feedback. For a paid event, reconcile receipts and refunds through authorized channels. Use the findings to improve the next broadcast rather than assuming every performance needs to be streamed.
