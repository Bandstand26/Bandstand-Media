Understanding Music Copyright and Social Media Monetization

BandStand Media · November 28, 2026

Marching band content often combines copyrighted compositions, custom arrangements and newly recorded performances. Each layer can involve different rights, and misunderstandings can lead to claims, muted videos or financial disputes.

Distinguish the rights

The underlying composition and a particular sound recording are separate works. An arrangement may also require authorization. Recording a live performance yourself does not automatically grant rights to the underlying composition or arrangement.

Understand online video

Pairing music with video can implicate synchronization rights, while distributing recordings or livestreaming can raise other licensing questions. A license for an in-person performance may not cover every online use. Ask the institution's licensing contact or qualified counsel about the specific activity.

Do not rely on myths

Giving credit, using fewer than thirty seconds or labeling a video educational does not automatically make a use lawful. Fair use is a context-specific legal analysis, not a fixed duration rule. Platform music libraries may also restrict commercial or off-platform use.

Build a clearance workflow

Record the title, composer, arranger, rights contacts, intended distribution, monetization status and any permission documents. Confirm whether replay, editing and promotional excerpts are covered. If clearance is uncertain, postpone publication or use appropriately licensed original material.

Respond to claims responsibly

Review the claim and consult the authorized rights contact. Do not file a dispute unless there is a legitimate basis. Keep correspondence and permissions organized, and avoid reposting disputed material simply to bypass enforcement.

Use authoritative resources

The U.S. Copyright Office provides introductory guidance on musical compositions, sound recordings and fair use at copyright.gov. Platform-specific rules should be checked directly before publication. This article is general educational information, not legal advice.
