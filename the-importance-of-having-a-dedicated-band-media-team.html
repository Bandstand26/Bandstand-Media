The Importance of Having a Dedicated Band Media Team

BandStand Media · November 28, 2026

A band director should not have to conduct rehearsals, manage logistics and personally edit every video. A dedicated media team can distribute the workload while giving students valuable experience in communication and production.

Define roles and authority

Assign a staff supervisor, camera operators, editors and a publishing lead. The supervisor controls final approval and safeguarding decisions. In a small program, one person may fill multiple roles, but responsibilities should still be documented.

Build a production workflow

Before an event, prepare a shot list and confirm permissions. Afterward, upload files to approved storage, select footage, edit, review and publish. Use naming conventions that include date, event and camera identifier so archives remain searchable.

Train for quality and safety

Teach framing, audio monitoring, caption accuracy and basic copyright awareness. Explain where filming is prohibited and how to respond to requests from students who should not appear. Student volunteers should never be expected to handle threatening messages independently.

Secure the accounts

Use institutional ownership, multifactor authentication and individual access where available. Avoid sharing passwords in group chats. Remove access promptly when a member leaves and keep recovery information with authorized staff.

Example: a game-week workflow

Monday: assign coverage. Thursday: confirm equipment and permissions. Friday: capture approved footage. Saturday: organize and edit. Monday: publish after review. The schedule can be adjusted around exams and rehearsal demands rather than imposing unrealistic turnaround times.

Develop succession

Keep a handbook and train incoming members alongside experienced editors. Evaluate the team on reliability, accuracy and contribution to program goals, not just views.
