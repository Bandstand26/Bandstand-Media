Using Social Media to Recruit Future Band Members

BandStand Media · November 28, 2026

Recruitment begins before a student walks into the band room. Social media can make participation feel understandable and attainable, especially for students whose families have never been involved in marching band.

Address uncertainty

Explain whether beginners can join, what instruments are available, how auditions work and what time commitments are expected. Avoid presenting only elite performers; that can unintentionally suggest that students must already be accomplished to participate.

Show the pathway

Create a series following the progression from first rehearsal to performance, using approved footage and student consent. Explain sectionals, uniform fittings and leadership opportunities. For college recruitment, direct students to official admissions, scholarship and audition information rather than promising awards.

Make information actionable

Publish dates, location, eligibility, preparation materials and an institutional contact in one accessible landing page. Use captions and readable text. Ensure students without social accounts can obtain the same information through the school.

Coordinate feeder programs

High school directors can work with middle school directors on approved joint events and instrument demonstrations. College programs can share audition clinics through school channels. Avoid collecting unnecessary personal information or contacting minors through private creator accounts.

Example: an eight-week campaign

Weeks one and two introduce sections; weeks three and four answer beginner questions; week five promotes an open rehearsal; week six explains auditions; weeks seven and eight provide reminders. Track inquiry forms, event attendance and actual enrollment separately to identify where students drop out.

Evaluate access and retention

Ask new members which information helped them decide and what remained unclear. Recruitment is not complete at registration: communicate equipment needs, rehearsal expectations and support resources to help students remain involved.

Band directors, auxiliary coaches and creators can learn about BandStand's marching band community and upcoming opportunities.
